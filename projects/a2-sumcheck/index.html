A2 · verifiable computation

Convincing someone of a sum they never add up

The sumcheck protocol, run one step at a time — press Next step.

Suppose you pay a data centre to run a job too big for your laptop, and it sends back an answer. Checking it the obvious way means doing the whole job again, which defeats the point, so in practice you simply trust the answer — and a machine that is broken, overcharging or lying looks exactly like one that is working.

This page runs the procedure that removes the trust. The side that did the work answers a short sequence of questions about it, and the side that paid for it can tell a correct answer from a wrong one while doing a tiny fraction of the arithmetic. It is what lets a blockchain accept a batch of transactions it never re-executes.

Can a liar get through?

A prover that lies has to guess, in advance, the random numbers the verifier will pick; it gets away with it only if a guess happens to land on one of a handful of unlucky values. With v rounds the chance of that is at most d·v / p. Run it and count: 200 provers, each lying about the total, each trying to bluff its way through.
