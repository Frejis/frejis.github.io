Latency distribution — the number that matters

Most orders are fast. The question that decides whether a matching engine is production-grade is not the average — it is how bad the slowest 1 in 1000 gets. The x-axis below is logarithmic because the interesting part of this distribution spans several orders of magnitude that a linear axis would crush into one spike.

Why "averaged over batches": browsers deliberately coarsen performance.now() (a side-channel mitigation) to a tick — often 0.1 ms — that a single order-book operation cannot clear. Timing one operation directly just measures the clock's own step function. So this page times batches of K operations with one performance.now() pair, divides by K, and takes one sample per batch — K is picked at runtime to comfortably clear the clock's measured resolution, shown below. The stats and chart are honestly labelled: they are per-operation cost averaged over each batch, with percentiles computed across batches — smoother than a true per-operation distribution, and unable to surface a genuine single-op outlier the way per-operation timing would if this clock allowed it.
