Writeup · A8

Differential privacy you can watch being spent

8 minute read Laplace mechanism · sensitivity · composition

Anonymised data is not anonymous. The interesting question is what a formal guarantee costs you, and this page charges you for it in front of the reader.

Open the demo

1The problem

"We only publish aggregates" is the standard defence of a data release, and it is not a defence. Two innocuous aggregate answers can differ by exactly one person, and that difference is that person's record. Explaining this in the abstract convinces nobody, because the attack sounds contrived until you see it executed on a database that looks entirely reasonable.

Differential privacy is the actual answer, and it is usually presented as a definition with an epsilon in it. What the definition does not convey is that the guarantee is about a whole sequence of questions, not any single one, and that it is therefore a finite resource which can be spent.

2What I built

A query dashboard over 2,000 synthetic Danes with an age, a region, an income and a chronic diagnosis. Ask aggregate questions — how many people have the diagnosis in Midtjylland, what the average income is in Nordjylland — and get an answer that has been deliberately blurred by a measured amount of noise. The true answer sits next to it, which no real deployment would ever show, because the gap between the two columns is what there is to learn.

A budget bar at the top drains with every query. When it is empty the database stops answering, and that refusal is the point of the whole design. There is also a switch that turns the guarantee off, and a differencing attack that then recovers one individual's diagnosis exactly. Turn the noise back on and the same attack degrades to a coin flip. Four charts, all computed live in the page, show the distribution of noisy answers, attack success against epsilon, error against epsilon with the theoretical curve overlaid, and a real analytical finding surviving the noise.

3How it works

The Laplace mechanism adds noise drawn from Laplace(0, Δ/ε) to a query's true answer, where Δ is the query's sensitivity: the most the answer can change when a single person is added or removed. That single quantity is the whole job, and getting it wrong produces a page that looks identical and guarantees nothing.

  • A count moves by at most 1 when one person joins, so Δ = 1 and noise of scale 1/ε suffices.
  • A sum over incomes has no finite sensitivity at all until the range is clamped, because one person with an unbounded income moves it arbitrarily far. The clamp is not tidying; it is what makes the query answerable, and the page says so when it bites.
  • A mean is two releases, so it splits its epsilon between a noisy sum and a noisy count.
  • A histogram over disjoint regions is charged once, not once per bar, because each person falls in exactly one bin. That is parallel composition, where the cost is the maximum rather than the sum.

Composition is what makes the budget meaningful. Sequential releases add their epsilons; disjoint ones take the maximum. The budget object refuses a query that would overrun the remaining allowance and, importantly, records no spend when it refuses — an off-by-one there would leak the last query for free.

The differencing attack is the concrete argument. Find a person uniquely identified by a combination of attributes, ask for the count of the diagnosis over the whole group, ask again over the group minus that person's defining predicate, and subtract. With noise off, the difference is 0 or 1 and is that individual's diagnosis. With noise on, the difference is dominated by two Laplace draws, and the chart of attack success against epsilon collapses onto the 50% line, which is where the attacker has learned nothing.

4What was hard

Proving the claims rather than asserting them, because everything in this area is statistical and a single run proves nothing in either direction. The tests do the work: the seeded generator is byte-identical across runs, Laplace samples are checked to have mean 0 and variance 2b² over 200,000 draws, an outlier's contribution to a clamped sum is shown to stay under the declared sensitivity, sequential composition is checked to sum and parallel composition to max, and the differencing attack is verified in both directions — exact with noise off, and back down to roughly a coin flip at ε = 0.05 over 1,500 seeded trials.

The corresponding design decision on the page is that a single attack run is never presented as evidence. The honest claim is about the distribution over many attempts, so the attack panel is backed by a chart of hundreds of runs at each epsilon rather than by one dramatic success.

5What it is not

  • The data is synthetic. 2,000 fictional records from a fixed seed. The regions are real administrative units; every number attached to them is invented.
  • The trusted-curator problem is not solved. This is the central model: the code holds raw records in memory and adds noise on output. Who is allowed to hold that data is a harder question than the mathematics here.
  • The Laplace implementation is the naive one. Inverse transform sampling on 64-bit floats leaks through gaps in the floating-point representation, a known result; production mechanisms use discrete Laplace or snapping variants.
  • Basic composition only. No advanced composition, no Rényi or zCDP accountant, no amplification by subsampling, so the budget spends faster than a serious deployment would need.
  • The true answer is on screen, deliberately, and only because this is a teaching tool. Shipping that column would defeat the entire mechanism.
  • The Gaussian mechanism uses the classical (ε, δ) analysis, valid for ε ≤ 1 and merely conservative above it. The slider goes to 5.
